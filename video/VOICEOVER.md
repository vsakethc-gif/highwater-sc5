# Voiceover script (version 2)

Record this **in one take** with your phone's Voice Memos app:
- Find a quiet room: no fan, TV, or other people talking. Hold the phone about a hand's width (20 cm) from your mouth.
- Read each numbered line, then **stop and count to two in your head** before the next one. Don't say the numbers.
- If you stumble, pause, then say the whole line again. I'll keep your last try.
- Talk a little slower than feels normal, like you're explaining it to a neighbor.
- When you're done, put the file in your Downloads folder (or `video/raw/`) and tell me. I cut it into the lines, clean the noise, even out the volume, and the video re-times itself to your voice.

Never say "safe," and don't say the app would have saved lives or predicted Helene.

---

**1 · Helene**
In September 2024, Hurricane Helene hit South Carolina.

**2 · The problem**
The warnings were out there. Alerts from the National Weather Service, river levels from USGS, flood maps from FEMA. But they were scattered across different websites, and written for experts.

**3**
That's where HighWater comes in.

**4 · Intro**
I'm Saketh, from York County, and this is HighWater. It shows people in South Carolina's 5th District the flood and weather dangers near them right now, and what to do about them. It's for everyone in our eight counties, especially older adults and families without a car.

**5 · Live status**
This is live data for York County. There are four levels: Normal, Watch, Warning, and Danger.

**6**
You can see every reason, with its source. The river gauges show how high the water is right now, and how it's been changing.

**7 · Scenario Mode**
To show what it looks like when things get bad, Scenario Mode replays a storm. It's always labeled Simulation.

**8**
As I drag the timeline, the whole screen changes with the danger, and so do the steps.

**9**
This replays the real alerts and river readings from the morning Helene arrived.

**10**
Here, the flood reaches Danger.

**11 · Tornado**
But in a tornado warning, driving away is the wrong advice. So there's no route out. It tells you to stay where you are and take shelter.

**12 · Map**
The district map brings it all together: warning areas, river gauges, flood zones, and where older adults and families without cars live. That way you can see where help matters most.

**13 · Report**
Anyone can report a problem, like a streetlight that's out or a flooded road. There's no account and no personal information. Reports are marked unverified, and the app points you to the official place to report it.

**14 · How it works**
HighWater doesn't use AI to make decisions. It uses written rules.

**15**
If the data doesn't load, it says it can't confirm, instead of guessing.

**16 · Built with**
I built HighWater with TypeScript, React Native, and Expo, using public data from the National Weather Service, USGS, NOAA, FEMA, and the Census.

**17 · What I learned**
What I learned is that the best apps are ones that are simple and solve important problems, not the ones that have thousands of different features.

**18 · End**
HighWater is not an official warning system. In an emergency, call 911.
